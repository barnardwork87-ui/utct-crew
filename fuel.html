Fuelling · Mikey L’s department

He sweats likeit’s a hobby.Feed him.

73 kg. Normally drinks 2 litres on a 15–20 km run, which tells you everything about his sweat. Salt and fluid will end his race before his legs do, so this page is mostly about stopping that.

Every hour, for about 19 hours

The four numbers.

0g

Carbs per hour

About 1 g per kg per hour. 70–90 g is the window. Under 60 and he bonks around Constantia.

0ml

Fluid per hour

600–900 ml depending on heat. More on the exposed coastal bits, less at night.

0mg

Sodium per hour

Heavy sweater, so 800–1,000 mg. If his cap has white salt crust on it, push to the top end.

0mg

Caffeine, total

About 3–4 mg/kg, all of it from Hout Bay onwards. The chocolate-coffee gels do the work.

General endurance guidelines for his bodyweight, not medical advice. Nothing new on race day. If it hasn’t been in his stomach on a long run, it doesn’t go in on Saturday.

The arsenal

The Näak stuff he already uses, plus what I’d add. Gel numbers are confirmed. Waffle and bar numbers are estimates until someone reads the packet out loud to me.

Station by station

What to hand him at each crew station to get him to the next one. The simple version: one flask of mix every hour, one gel or solid every hour, one salt cap every 40 minutes. Real food at the table is a bonus on top.

Caffeine strategy

No caffeine in the morning. He doesn’t need it on Platteklip and he’ll need it a lot more at 9pm in Newlands Forest. The chocolate-coffee gels start at Hout Bay, then Coke at Alphen and Newlands.

When it goes wrong

SignsQuiet, grumpy, slow

He’s bonking. Give him a gel immediately, plus Coke if you’ve got it. Don’t ask him if he wants it. Ten minutes later he’ll be a person again.

SignsSloshy stomach, nausea

Too much fluid without salt, or he’s overheating. Pause the drinking, give him salt, ginger, sips of Coke, and walk him out of the station. Don’t force food.

SignsNot peeing, or dark pee

He’s behind on fluid and salt. One extra flask and two salt caps over the next hour.

SignsCramping

Usually salt plus tired muscles. Salt cap, pickle juice if you’ve got some, and walk the next climb.

SignsPuffy hands, confused

Possibly too much water and not enough salt. That’s a medic conversation, not a crew one. Find race medical.

Signs“I’m fine”

Classic symptom. Ignore it and keep following the plan.

The finish

After that, he caneat whatever he wants.
